World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in Paris that the US economic campaign against Iran was effective enough that "there's really no need for major combat operations any longer" (New York Times). Iran's Revolutionary Guards said three oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines, and monitors counted at least 37 attacks on ships in and near the Strait of Hormuz in November, the most since the war began.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called the first day of Ukraine-Russia-US talks in Abu Dhabi "constructive and quite substantive"; officials said the sides agreed a large prisoner-of-war exchange. NATO scrambled jets overnight after at least four drones strayed into Baltic states' airspace during strikes on Russia.

The Democratic Republic of the Congo confirmed 11,042 Ebola cases and at least 5,387 deaths, the Bundibugyo-strain outbreak now the second largest and second deadliest on record, the government said Tuesday.

Israel's new parliament was sworn in without a government after a deadlocked election stripped Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of his majority; Israeli forces killed at least seven people in Gaza, including a father and his 13-year-old son, local health officials said.

At COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, UN chief Antonio Guterres told leaders the slow response to climate change had put the world "on a path to destruction". The US House voted 211-204 to send the impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, and House Republicans elected Jim Jordan as minority leader.

Anthropic filed for a Nasdaq listing that could value it at $1.4 trillion, shareholders sued Nvidia and CEO Jensen Huang over a Chinese spying scandal, and US consumer prices rose 0.4% last month, making a December rate rise likely. Salvadoran forces destroyed a Haitian gang leader's house and the 400 Mawozo gang's headquarters, killing 20 people, President Nayib Bukele said.
